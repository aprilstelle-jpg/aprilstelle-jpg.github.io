Electric Vehicles in Washington State

State incentives and popular electric vehicles in Washington State

By April Stelle

As of Oct. 1 of last year, federal rebates of up to $7,500 for new electric vehicles, meant to last through 2032, were cancelled as a part of the Trump administration's “big beautiful bill.” This change at the federal level has left tax incentives and rebates for EVs up to states. Transportation accounts for the largest percentage of all US greenhouse gas emissions with about 29% of all emissions. Highway vehicles release about 1.5 million tons of greenhouse gases every year. A study from USC’s Keck School of Medicine found that for 200 EVs added, nitrogen dioxide levels went down by 1.1%.

Washington is one of the leading states in the adoption of electric vehicles. As of 2023, 2.67% of all vehicles in the state were electric. Its' per capita EV registration rate is top three in the country, only behind California and Colorado. In Washington, federal rebates known as Clean Vehicle Credit, ended when the federal funding was stopped. Despite this, there has been a steady increase in the population of EVs in Washington state over the last five years. Washington state established the Interagency Electric Vehicle Coordinating Council, also referred to as the EV Council, in 2022. The council is made up of 10 state agencies who work to increase statewide access to EVs and reliable charging stations. EVs have been linked to lowering greenhouse gas emissions that are released by tailpipe exhaust in gas engine vehicles. A study from USCs' Keck School of Medicine found that for 200 EVs added, nitrogen dioxide levels went down by 1.1%.

Since the councils' establishment, there have been multiple rebates and incentives enacted in Washington state. The Low-Income Electric Vehicle Rebates was enacted in October 2024. allowed applicants with a household income 300% below the federal poverty level to get a rebate of up to $9,000 on new EVs and $2,000 on used EVs. The program ended in 2024 due to lack of funds. Also in 2024, over $7.6 million of federal grant funding went toward Electric Vehicle Charger Reliability Grants. It was estimated that these grants would replace 66 charging ports while adding 44 new charging ports to 13 communities.

There are two main kinds of EVs; Battery Electric Vehicles, or BEVs, and Plug-in Hybrid Vehicles (PHEVs). BEVs are fully electric vehicles that do not use gas at all to power the vehicle. PHEVs have an electric battery and a gas tank which kicks in when the electric battery runs out. Adam Jester, founder and CEO of Bamboo Strategy Group, a business consulting firm that focuses largely on electric vehicle consulting, said PHEVs seem like a way for consumers to “dip their toes in the water” of electric vehicles without giving up the familiarity of the internal combustion engine, or ICE, which are the fully gas engines consumers have experience with.

“‘I still have the safety net that I've always had of this gas engine, and that makes me comfortable, and I still feel like I'm doing something good for the environment,’ and that's great, certainly that's better than driving an eight mile to the gallon ICE vehicle,” Jester said. “But you're also getting the worst of both worlds, because you're not getting a ton of range full electric and you still have tailpipes, which means you have tailpipe emissions.”

Despite these concerns, the population of BEVs in Washington state is consistently higher than PHEVs. In a BEV, there are no tailpipes and therefore no carbon emissions. The rebates targeted the concern surrounding the affordability of fully electric vehicles. This is mostly due to the cost of making the lithium-ion batteries, which are made of metals like nickel, cobalt, lithium and manganese. In 2024, manufacturers spent just over $10,000 per battery, according to the U.S. Department of Energy. While this is still a hefty figure, it is about $4,000 lower than the $14,000 price in 2019. Jester said the current advancements in battery technology should make it more affordable and lead to overall lower prices down the line.

Another factor is the manufacturer of EVs. Teslas are the most popular EV on the market in Washington state with their population beating out the runner-up Chevrolet six-times over. The 2023 Model Y was their most popular model with 59,865 vehicles sold from 2023 to 2026. Chevrolets' Bolt EV model sold just over 7,000. In 2023, Tesla's Model Y was the best-selling vehicle in the world.

Jester owned a Tesla Model 3 before switching to a Mustang. He said the technology in the Tesla was far superior to any other EV on the market, and that is what made it so popular.

“Tesla got out in front of EVs. They committed to EVs over a decade before most other American Original Equipment Manufacturers did. And so they tried it, they failed at it, they fixed it, and then they improved upon it, and they have done that for the last 10 years or more,” Jester said. “While they were doing that, everyone else was dabbling at best, or just openly laughing at Tesla for their failures. Very quietly, they just developed this far superior machine.”

China has the largest population of EVs in the world and manufactures 70% of the global production in 2024. Earlier this month, China manufacturers unveiled EVs with AI software and autonomous driving capabilities with the goal of making EVs "self reasoning machines." This comes at the same time as a bipartisan bill was introduced by Sens. Bernie Moreno, R-Ohio, and Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich., that would ban imports of vehicles and autoparts made in China. The bill is meant to protect the US auto industry, but it could also have a negative impact on the EV market since so many EVs and EV parts are made in China.

The future of rebates and incentives for EVs in the US are uncertain. Since the Trump administration's cancellation of federal funding and incentives, Jester said the future of EVs is now up to individual states, which Jester predicts will lean more towards charging incentives rather than rebates for the vehicles themselves.

"It's disappointing that the rest of the world is going the opposite direction," Jester said. "The other other countries are innovating. They're getting really good at making EVs and selling them and marketing them and charging them and we're kind of going in the opposite direction, which is unfortunate."
